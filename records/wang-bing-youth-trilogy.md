# Wang Bing — *Youth* trilogy geography

## Source facts

Wang Bing's *Youth* trilogy — *Youth (Spring)*, *Youth (Hard Times)*, and *Youth (Homecoming)* — is centered on the garment industry of Zhili Town (织里镇), Wuxing District, Huzhou City, Zhejiang Province, China.

Harvard Film Archive states that Wang Bing and his crew shot about 2,600 hours of footage between 2014 and 2019 in Zhili, a garment-making township near Shanghai. It describes hundreds of thousands of seasonal laborers working in roughly 18,000 workshops.

The Cannes press material for *Youth (Spring)* says Wang intended the project to focus on Zhili and then "radiate out" to workers' home towns and villages. Wang describes traveling as much as 2,000 km upriver and later filming workers in home villages in Anhui and Yunnan. He also describes the typical Zhili workshop as having fewer than twenty workers and notes that workers may arrive in groups drawn from the same family or village.

*Youth (Homecoming)* makes this outward geography explicit. La Biennale's synopsis traces the New Year migration from Zhili and the Yangtze basin to the mountains of Yunnan. Icarus Films likewise describes the film beginning with the end of the work season in Zhili, following workers back to their families and weddings, and then following them back to Zhili after the holiday.

## Geographic structure

The trilogy therefore supplies a concrete two-direction geographic record:

- **industrial destination:** Zhili, Wuxing District, Huzhou, Zhejiang;
- **worker-origin regions explicitly documented by Wang:** villages in Anhui and Yunnan;
- **movement:** rural home villages → Zhili for seasonal garment work → home villages for New Year → Zhili again.

Zhili itself is not simply a large vertically integrated factory complex. The sources describe a dense network of small workshops, commonly with fewer than twenty workers, linked to a very large migrant labor population.

## Use in `privilege`

This is useful for the project's nested-geography model because it records the same relative structure in the opposite direction from examples that begin with affluent places, firms, or executive hierarchies.

The direction should not be treated as a different model. If privilege level is represented schematically by an exponent, moving toward less privileged positions is the same hierarchy with a negative change in exponent.

The films also connect several scales without collapsing them:

1. an individual worker;
2. a workshop of roughly tens of workers;
3. Zhili's garment-production cluster;
4. migrant-source villages and provinces;
5. the wider regional relation between the lower Yangtze industrial economy and poorer inland/rural origin areas.

The films are evidence for the locations, movements, workshop organization, and lived working conditions they document. Any numeric privilege exponent, percentile placement, or comparison with US examples remains a derived project construction rather than a claim made by Wang Bing or the cited institutions.

## Evidence boundary

The cited sources support Zhili as the trilogy's central production location, the 2014–2019 filming period, the roughly 2,600 hours of footage, the small-workshop structure, and the documented journeys to workers' home regions including Anhui and Yunnan. They do not by themselves establish a single quantitative wealth or privilege gradient between every origin village and Zhili, nor do they establish equivalence with any US locality used elsewhere in this project.
